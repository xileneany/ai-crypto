Top 5 stories of the week

01
ETF flows May 25–29
$1.47B pulled from crypto ETFs — IBIT records its 2nd-largest single-day outflow ever at $528M
Digital asset investment products recorded $1.47B in net outflows for the week — the third-largest weekly exit of all of 2026, according to CoinShares. Bitcoin funds alone shed $1.32B, the largest single-week BTC ETF outflow of the year. The 11 US-listed spot funds saw $1.26B depart — following the prior week's $1B exodus. The worst single day came on May 28: BlackRock IBIT recorded $528M in outflows — the second-largest daily outflow in the fund's history. A confirmed $1.29B block trade in IBIT at 10:30am drew widespread attention, though price absorbed it without a cascade. XRP ETFs bucked the trend completely, posting $35M in inflows while BTC and ETH funds lost $2B combined over the two weeks. Cumulative 2026 net inflows across all Bitcoin ETFs have now shrunk to just $536M — from roughly $2.1B a month ago. Jane Street and Goldman Sachs both reduced BTC ETF exposure in Q1. The institutional bid that drove BTC to $81K in early May has paused.
02
Macro pressure Week of May 25
Treasuries signal "higher for longer" — US-Iran Strait of Hormuz escalation hits risk appetite
The double pressure on crypto this week came from two directions. First: the bond market. Following Moody's May 16 downgrade of US sovereign credit from Aaa to Aa1 — the first in 109 years — rising Treasury yields sent the signal that rate cuts are further away than hoped. When Treasuries compete for capital at higher yields, leveraged risk assets suffer most. Second: geopolitical escalation. US-Iran tensions over the Strait of Hormuz — the chokepoint through which 20% of global oil flows — spiked oil prices and crushed risk appetite. The combination pushed BTC to $74,192 intraday on May 23 before the Iran deal bounce. Bitcoin's weakness this week was notably crypto-specific: the S&P 500 held up far better on a relative basis, suggesting the institutional exit was BTC-targeted, not a broad risk-off rotation. BTC's 84% correlation to equities is only hurting it when the correlation runs negative — and this week, equities outperformed.
03
On-chain signal May 29–31
Record 15.8M BTC in long-term holder wallets — RSI hits 27.93, deepest oversold since January
While ETF investors sold aggressively, on-chain data told the opposite story. Long-term holder supply reached a record 15.8 million BTC according to CryptoQuant — coins not moved in 155+ days. This metric historically peaks before recoveries, not before structural crashes. The RSI hit 27.93 on May 29 — the deepest oversold reading in four months. The last comparable print in late January 2026 preceded a +11% bounce over the following 6 sessions. Order book data reveals a $443M buy wall of limit orders near $70,000 — the realized price for the 1–3 month cohort, a psychologically loaded level where recent buyers begin to feel real pain. Adam Back flagged Bitcoin's 200-week moving average crossing $61,000 as "a structural bull signal" — citing historical precedent where BTC trading above this level has always preceded recovery. The data is bullish long-term even when the price action is not.
04
Regulation May 29
CFTC approves the first US-regulated Bitcoin perpetual futures — Coinbase and Robinhood stocks surge
In the middle of a brutal week for prices, the CFTC greenlit the first ever regulated Bitcoin perpetual futures contract in the US, to be listed by KalshiEX LLC. Coinbase and Robinhood stocks surged on the news — a reminder that the regulatory infrastructure for crypto continues to be built even when prices are falling. Bitcoin perpetual futures are the most-traded crypto instrument globally, but have historically operated offshore or in grey zones. A US-regulated perp creates a compliant on-ramp for institutions and hedge funds that previously couldn't access the instrument without compliance risk. This is a slow-burn catalyst, not an immediate price driver. But combined with the CLARITY Act targeting a June–July floor vote and GENIUS Act implementation rules due July 18, the regulatory stack for the next cycle is being assembled while prices fall.
05
Market structure May 31
CME's first gap-free Monday arrives June 1 — a decade-long trading signal just went silent forever
Bitcoin entered June 2026 with a structural change: CME launched 24/7 Bitcoin futures trading, permanently eliminating weekend price gaps. For years, "CME gap fills" were one of the most reliable short-term trading signals in crypto — prices almost always returned to close the voids left by weekend moves. That signal is now structurally gone. The first gap-free Monday in history arrives June 1. Traders who leaned on gap-fills for entries and confirmations must adapt. In a bearish environment, the loss of a reliable technical signal adds uncertainty. Bitcoin closes May at $73,751 — its lowest monthly close since February. The weekly chart shows a broken consolidation below all major moving averages. Market structure is bearish. The longs need a catalyst; the shorts need the $70K wall to crack.
Key tension

Oversold on-chain vs. persistent institutional exit — the BTC paradox of May 2026

The bear case and the bull case are both backed by real data — simultaneously
Here is the honest read of this week: two opposing forces have never been more clearly at war in Bitcoin's market structure.

The bear case is ETF flows. Nine consecutive days of outflows. $1.47B in a single week. BlackRock's $528M single-day exit. The institutional demand that drove BTC from $65K to $81K in April and early May has visibly paused. The macro backdrop — rising yields, Moody's downgrade, geopolitical risk premium — is not supportive of new institutional allocations. Without the bid, BTC can't sustain a rally. And the S&P 500 is outperforming BTC on a week-to-week basis, which means the "digital gold" narrative isn't yet capturing mainstream flight capital.

The bull case is on-chain. 15.8M BTC in long-term holder wallets — a record. RSI at 27.93 — deepest oversold since January (which preceded +11% bounce). $443M buy wall at $70K. The 200-week SMA holding at $61K. CoinLore's composite of 23 indicators tilts bearish short-term but bullish over 30–60 days. And Fidelity's Jurrien Timmer's model explicitly calls 2026 an "off year" with support in the $65K–$75K range — before the next leg.

The resolution likely comes from outside crypto: the CLARITY Act floor vote, a Warsh June FOMC signal, or Iran deal finalization will be the catalyst that breaks the standoff. Until then, the range is $70K–$78K, and the winner is whoever can wait longest.
The structural read
Despite this week's carnage, cumulative ETF net inflows since launch sit at $57.1B across all funds. Total net assets: $98.9B. IBIT holds 4% of Bitcoin's entire circulating supply. The institutional position has not been liquidated — it has been paused. The moment yields stabilize or a macro catalyst arrives, the marginal bid returns to a market where supply is structurally tight. Long-term holders holding a record 15.8M BTC means the float available to new buyers is historically small. When the bid returns, there is very little to buy.
