CLARITY Act May 14
CLARITY Act clears Senate Banking Committee 15–9 — two Democrats cross the aisle
After months of negotiation, the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act passed out of the Senate Banking Committee in a 15–9 bipartisan vote on May 14 — a historic first for US crypto legislation. Two Democrats voted in favor, making the committee result more durable than the party-line 13–11 that advanced Warsh. Full CLARITY Act text was released at midnight May 12 by Chairman Tim Scott: 309 pages covering token classification, trading platform oversight, stablecoin rules, DeFi protocol accountability, and Treasury-CFTC co-rulemaking authority. Key compromises: fan tokens reclassified as digital collectibles, DeFi developers receive legal protections, and stablecoin yield is banned if "functionally equivalent" to bank deposits (but activity-based rewards are permitted). Senator Elizabeth Warren led opposition, filing 40+ of the 100+ amendments expected before the Senate floor vote. The ethics provision — limiting government officials from profiting on crypto — remains unresolved. The CLARITY Act now heads to the full Senate. If the 60-vote threshold holds, it reaches Trump's desk this summer.
02
Macro May 13–15
Treasury yields hit multi-year highs as inflation data stalls — Bitcoin retreats from $82K to $78K
The week's defining market dynamic: the 2-year Treasury yield hit a 12-month high above 4.05%, the 10-year broke above 4.55%, and the 30-year sold at a 5% yield for the first time since 2007. The trigger was hotter-than-expected inflation data, combined with markets repricing the Fed path under incoming Chair Warsh. The move created a classic risk-off cascade: Bitcoin opened Monday at $82,164 — the strongest opening since January 31 — then retreated steadily throughout the week to close near $78,225 by Friday. ETF investors treated rising yields as a sell signal: spot Bitcoin ETFs shed over $1 billion in net outflows across the week, ending a six-week inflow streak as BlackRock, Fidelity, and Grayscale all recorded significant redemptions. The inverse head-and-shoulders pattern in 2-year yields signals further upside pressure ahead. BTC now absorbs ETF buying at 10:1 demand-to-supply ratio (4,500–5,000 BTC/day vs 450 mined), but that structural support weakened meaningfully this week.
03
Fed May 14
Kevin Warsh confirmed as Fed Chair — crypto's most literate central banker takes the helm May 15
The US Senate confirmed Kevin Warsh as Fed Chair on May 14 after the Banking Committee's 13–11 party-line vote on April 29. Senator Fetterman crossed the aisle to provide bipartisan cover on the floor vote. Warsh officially replaced Powell on May 15. His profile is unprecedented for a sitting Fed Chair: called Bitcoin "new gold for people under 40," described digital assets as "already part of the fabric of our financial services industry," and has personal investment history including stakes in Bitwise, Electric Capital, Polychain Capital, and Polymarket (all of which he must divest). His first FOMC meeting is June 16–17. Trump has publicly stated he would be "disappointed" if Warsh doesn't cut in June. But Warsh's own record is hawkish: he called the 2022 inflation surge the Fed's "biggest policy mistake in 40–50 years." The market's immediate read: higher yields, not rate-cut euphoria. J.P. Morgan maintains Warsh will push for cuts faster than Powell — but the data must cooperate first. Historical pattern: BTC has corrected for 2–3 months after each Fed Chair transition before rallying.

Security May 16
THORChain exploited for $10.7M across four chains — RUNE drops 12%, protocol halts trading
ZachXBT flagged a $10.7M exploit on THORChain spanning four separate chains on May 16. The cross-chain liquidity protocol responded by halting all trading and posting a chain halt. RUNE dropped 12% within hours of the announcement. This is THORChain's second significant security incident in 12 months. Separately, Binance Research released its 2026 illicit finance report: $75 billion in illicit funds sat on-chain in 2025, up 28% year-on-year — yet still under 1% of total crypto volume. The report found stablecoins now drive 84% of illicit transactions, while Bitcoin's share has fallen to just 7%. On the hardware side, Ledger and Trezor jointly announced support for Ethereum's new Clear Signing standard — a direct response to the blind-signing scam epidemic. The week also saw Bitwise list BHYP on the NYSE, the first US ETF offering direct exposure to Hyperliquid's HYPE token with built-in staking yield (0.67% fee, custodied by Anchorage Digital).

Stablecoins May 17
A major bank moves stablecoin reserves into Wall Street's repo market — 67M Americans now hold crypto
CryptoSlate reported on May 17 that a major unnamed US bank moved its stablecoin reserve strategy into the repo market — Wall Street's $10T-a-day short-term financing machine — using T-bills as the collateral layer. This is stablecoin infrastructure embedding itself directly into traditional financial plumbing, enabled by CLARITY Act momentum. Separately, the National Cryptocurrency Association's 2026 State of Crypto Holders Report found 67 million Americans now own digital assets — up 12 million in a year, meaning 1 in 4 US adults participates in the crypto economy. Among 2025–2026 new buyers: 42% are female, 26% are Gen X, and 13% are Boomers. Four in 10 holders now actively use crypto in daily transactions. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve: officials confirmed a "major announcement coming within weeks" — 13F filings on May 15 showed which institutions bought crypto-linked securities in Q1, with multiple new institutional names appearing for the first time.
Key tension

The most important week for US crypto policy — and Bitcoin still fell

The CLARITY Act paradox: best regulatory news in years, worst price week in six
For months, markets closely watched five catalysts: the CLARITY Act, Warsh's nomination, Iran tensions, inflation data, and ETF flows. This week, three of the five broke in Bitcoin's favor — CLARITY Act advanced, Warsh was confirmed, Iran-US talks didn't collapse. And BTC still fell 4.3% from $82K to $78K while ETFs bled $1B.

The macro overrode everything. Rising Treasury yields — especially the 30-year hitting 5% for the first time since 2007 — are doing more damage than regulatory progress can offset in the short term. Higher yields make bonds competitive with risk assets. They signal inflation persistence. They reduce the expected probability and timing of Fed cuts. And BTC trades at 84% correlation with the S&P 500 — it cannot escape the macro gravity field, regardless of what happens in Washington.

The honest read: the regulatory thesis is intact and strengthening. The price thesis requires lower yields and/or a macro catalyst that reduces bond attractiveness. Warsh's June 16–17 FOMC is the next scheduled catalyst. If he cuts — under political pressure from Trump — rates fall and the dollar weakens. That's the unlock. If he holds or sounds hawkish, the $76,500 support gets tested before anything else.
The signal inside the noise
Despite $1B in ETF outflows, Bitcoin ETFs are still absorbing 4,500–5,000 BTC per day against only 450 mined daily — a 10:1 demand-to-supply ratio. This week's outflows represent profit-taking and yield-chasing, not structural institutional abandonment. The 67M American crypto holders figure and 13F filings showing new institutional entrants confirm the adoption story is still running. The price weakness is a macro side-effect, not a crypto-specific rejection.
